Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Arlington

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
