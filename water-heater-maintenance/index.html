If you could see inside your water heater, the case for maintenance would make itself. On the bottom there is a bed of mineral sediment, sometimes several inches deep, that makes the burner work harder, creates popping and rumbling, and can burn out the lower element on an electric unit. Hanging from the top is the anode rod, meant to corrode first, which may be down to bare wire after a few years. When it is used up, corrosion goes after the tank lining, and a leak is only a matter of time. For homeowners in Mansfield, TX, a yearly visit is the simplest way to keep both of those problems from shortening the life of the heater.

Our maintenance visit is built to interrupt both problems. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we test the elements and thermostats with a meter and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually setting off error codes. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the inlet screen, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. You get a straightforward report after every visit, noting what is healthy and what needs attention. Call (682) 310-2339 to book maintenance for your Mansfield water heater.

Signs You Need Water Heater Maintenance in Mansfield

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

Professional maintenance also brings the right parts on the truck, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Beyond the tools, experience matters: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Keep the thermostat near 120 degrees to limit scalding and slow sediment buildup
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
